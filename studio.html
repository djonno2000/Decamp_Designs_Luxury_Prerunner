The Studio

Build it like
you'd weld it.

A FIFO fabricator in the Adelaide Hills with a drift/JDM background. The trophy-truck build is the prototype and the proof piece — everything here runs on the same belief: the engineering is the design, and you don't buy a fix for a problem a clean design never has.

How it's built

First principles, budget-clever

Design from scratch

Fabrication is the edge, so it gets used — build the front geometry and the panel envelope rather than correcting production parts. Off-the-shelf long-tubes are the lazy call.

Spend where it matters

Money goes to what defines how it works and survives — chassis, suspension geometry, the shock package, hubs, brakes, safety. Everything else is sourced cheap and proven.

Used race take-offs

Trophy-truck teams cycle out strong used hardware — bypass shocks, steering boxes, hubs, take-off tyres. Used King take-offs at the common 14" front stroke get you top-shelf damping without new-price.

Honest about the catch

That take-off market is mostly US; from SA you wear freight, duty and lead time. The real lever is relationships in the small AU desert-race pool — not assuming US bargains.

Spend on what defines the car

  • Spaceframe & chassis stiffness
  • Suspension geometry & the shock package
  • Hubs, brakes, steering
  • Safety gear — cage, seats, harness, fire

Save on the ancillaries

  • Cooling within reason (derate UP for a blown LS)
  • Brake assist & donor-part hacks
  • Consumables, fasteners, sundries
  • Anything that doesn't change how it finishes

// the Giannini lesson: a $165 AC Delco radiator does the job a $1,500 trick unit does — but a supercharged LS dumps far more heat than an NA, so cooling gets derated up, not copied across.

The panel business

Tool once, two price points

One plug serves two products: thermoform off it for volume, and hand-lay composite off the same plug for one-off and structural jobs. The moat isn't the panel — it's compliance-correct geometry.

Flares that actually pass

A generator that draws the ADR-42-compliant guard envelope around any tyre — coverage angles, lip depth, width checks. Most imports don't pass; these are compliant by construction (geometry, not the certificate).

Materials on first principles

ABS/ASA for flares that take hits and paint well (ASA for unpainted sun exposure); TPO where customers want OEM-flex and accept the paint process.

Where the money is

4WD flares are the bread-and-butter; drift/JDM overfenders are high-margin and lean on the JDM network; Aussie classics build the brand. The trophy truck is the billboard, not the income.

No hype about the risk

Every design is a plug, so nothing gets tooled before demand is validated. Compete on compliance, fit, turnaround and local supply — and phase the capital, starting with what can be built.
