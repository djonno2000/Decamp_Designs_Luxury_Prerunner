Boosting an LS to live, not to grenade

The cheap forced-induction paths for an LS3, the junkyard LSA blower, and the honest question a desert truck has to answer first: does the prerunner even need boost — or does a well-built naturally aspirated LS finish more races?

trophy-truck ls-v8forced-inductionsuperchargerreliabilitynaturally-aspirated ⚑ Engine choice (NA vs blown) is an open decision

Roots over centrifugal, every time, for an easy install

If it's going to be blown, the install drives the choice. An Eaton TVS roots/PD blower (Magnuson, Edelbrock E-Force, Harrop, or the factory LSA/LS9 unit) sits in the valley with a short air path: instant low-end torque, minimal plumbing. A centrifugal (ProCharger/Vortech) is cheaper to buy but it's "a belt-driven turbo" — it needs charge piping and an intercooler and lags below ~3,500 rpm. For a truck that wants torque off idle in the rough, skip it.

  • New complete kits are the expensive path — Magnuson / E-Force / Whipple / ProCharger / Vortech run ~US$6–8k+ for the kit (~$10k+ AUD landed).
  • Junkyard hero = the LSA blower (Eaton TVS1900, 1.9 L) off a wrecked Cadillac CTS-V Gen2 or Camaro ZL1 — factory GM roots, OEM-durable, good for ~9 psi.
  • The forum catch: a bare blower is cheap, but the small bits (brackets, snout, lid, intercooler, water pump, injectors, clips) eat the budget and time. Fix: buy a complete LSA take-out — whole supercharged engine + accessories, often barely more money.
  • Clean Aussie bolt-on: Harrop FDFI2300 (Melbourne) — same Eaton TVS, intercooled, designed for L76/L98/LS2/LS3; Harrop pitches ~5 psi to keep stock internals happy. Best value-vs-hassle is a used complete roots kit off the AU market (VE/VF HSV guys part them out).

The LS3-specific catch: compression

An LS3 is 10.7:1 — detonation-prone under boost. On stock internals keep boost low (~5–7 psi), run a good intercooler, a careful tune and 98 octane. The stock forged crank and heads are safe to roughly 7–9 psi / ~550–600 hp with proper fuelling and cooling; past that you're into forged pistons. (An LS1 is the weaker base — cathedral-port, ~10.25:1 — so the LS3 is the better blown choice.) Cheapest proper blown base is a junkyard LY6/L92 (rectangle ports match the LSA intake with no adapter) + VVT delete + a blower cam + LS9 head gaskets. Watch the crank flange: LSA is 8-bolt, LS9 9-bolt, normal LS 6-bolt.

  • Power to expect: a mild bolt-on roots adds ~80–125 hp → LS3 ~430 → ~520–560 hp; an LSA blower lands ~550–600 hp. Modest, reliable, "safe stock-limit, a bit more competitive."
  • Supporting bits regardless: bigger injectors, a confirmed fuel pump, a proper tune, intercooling, colder gapped-down plugs, upgraded valve springs. No bottom-end teardown for a mild low-boost bump.

The honest question: does the prerunner even need boost?

A blower adds heat and cooling demand to an already cooling-challenged desert vehicle, plus weight and another failure point a long way from the workshop. A stock NA LS3 is already 430 hp. A good cam + intake + the NA-tuned exhaust (stepped primaries + X-pipe + megaphone) + a tune gets you to roughly 480–500 hp naturally aspirated — with zero boost complexity, less heat and better reliability. For a build whose north star is finishing, that's often the smarter competitive dollar.

Bottom line: cheapest-with-sourcing-effort is a junkyard LSA take-out; cheapest-with-zero-fab is a used complete Harrop/Magnuson kit (roots, low boost, intercooled, tuned, on 98). But genuinely consider a well-built NA LS3 — and if you go NA, the exhaust is where the real power hides.

Key takeaways

  • If blown: roots, not centrifugal — instant torque, simple install. The junkyard LSA take-out or a used AU roots kit beats a new kit on value.
  • LS3 is 10.7:1 — keep boost low (~5–7 psi), intercool, tune on 98; ~550–600 hp on stock internals before forged pistons.
  • A blower is heat + complexity on a cooling-challenged desert truck — real costs for a finishing-focused build.
  • A well-built NA LS3 (cam + intake + stepped/megaphone exhaust + tune) makes ~480–500 hp with none of that — frequently the smarter race dollar.
